The homework section involves text mining analysis of the Reddit Stock Sentiment Dataset. Before starting, I loaded the data using `load_dataset_tool` (result ID: load_dataset_1). The dataset contains 847 text entries, with 423 Neutral (0.0), 315 Negative (-1.0), and 109 Positive (1.0) entries, showing a significant imbalance in the amount of data across these three categories. Next, I used `check_missing_tool` (check_missing_3) to confirm the absence of missing values and `check_duplicates_tool` (check_duplicates_4) to identify 23 duplicate entries, which I did not delete in this analysis. Additionally, `describe_data_tool` (describe_data_5) shows that the average text length is approximately 148.68 characters, the median is 79 characters, and the maximum is as high as 2753 characters. This can be interpreted as most posts being relatively short, but a few still containing very long texts. The text length distribution results can be found in `../plots/李宥寬_homework_plot_describe_data_5.png`.

Therefore, to reduce the computational burden of subsequent text analysis, I used `sample_data_tool` (sample_data_6) to randomly sample 500 data points with a random state of 42 for subsequent analysis. After using `tokenize_tool` (tokenize_7), a total of 15,823 tokens were obtained. Then, `build_dtm_tool` (dtm_8) was used to build a Document-Term Matrix, containing 500 documents and 3,263 terms, with a sparsity of 99.3738%, indicating that the matrix formed by textual data is very sparse. `term_frequency_tool` (term_freq_9) showed that the most frequently occurring terms included common English words such as "the," "to," "and," and "is," which are the "stopwords" mentioned in the Master Question. Among them, "market," which is more relevant to the dataset's theme, appeared 66 times. This indicates that without further processing of stopwords, important terms that were originally more relevant to the documents would have been overlooked.

For feature analysis, I used variance, Pearson correlation, and Spearman correlation for feature filtering (`result IDs: variance_filter_11, pearson_filter_12, spearman_filter_13`). Since the threshold was set to 0, all 3,263 terms were retained, and no features were removed. Next, the `feature_correlation_matrix_tool` (feature_correlation_14) was used to analyze the correlation between 20 terms, including "the," "and," "market," and "star," generating a correlation matrix chart `../plots/李宥寬_homework_plot_feature_correlation_14.png`. These results allowed me to observe the associations between different textual features and showed that the threshold setting directly affects the feature selection results.

Next, for the Positive (1.0) category, I used `mine_patterns_tool` to perform FP-Growth frequent pattern mining (result ID: patterns_15), with the minimum support set to 5, finding a total of 8 frequent patterns. For example, "good" has a support of 9, "market" 6, and "technology" 5, while "just" has the highest support of 12. These results show that articles with positive sentiment do indeed contain some semantically common terms, such as "good" and "market," but they also contain more general terms like "just" and "going." Therefore, further stopword filtering or more complete text preprocessing might yield more representative patterns.

Finally, I used `reduce_dimensions_tool` in conjunction with PCA to reduce the text features to two dimensions (result ID: reduce_dimensions_16). The first principal component explains 23.70% of the variance, and the second principal component explains 6.53%, for a combined total of approximately 30.23%. From the PCA plot `../plots/李宥寬_homework_plot_reduce_dimensions_16.png`, it can be observed that the three sentiment categories still have significant overlap, indicating that the first two principal components alone are insufficient to clearly distinguish between Negative, Neutral, and Positive documents. Furthermore, `binarize_labels_tool` (binarize_labels_17) converts the three sentiment labels from 500 documents into a 500 × 3 one-hot encoded matrix, while `cosine_similarity_tool` (cosine_similarity_18) calculates the cosine similarity between Document 0 and Document 1 to be 0.0, indicating that these two documents have no overlapping terms in the current DTM representation.
